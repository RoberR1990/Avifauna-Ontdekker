## kind

This swan is black with a bright red beak. But look closely: when he spreads his wings, you see white feathers! He eats plants that grow under water. That is why his neck is so long.

## b1

The black swan comes from Australia. It lives at lakes, swamps and rivers with lots of water plants. Its feathers are black, except for the white flight feathers. Its beak is red with a pale band. It eats almost only plants, such as water plants and algae. The female usually lays 4 to 8 eggs, which are incubated for 35 to 48 days. The species is doing well. People have also released it in other countries, for example in New Zealand and Europe.

## kenner

Cygnus atratus is 110 to 142 cm long, weighs 3,700 to 9,000 g and has a wingspan of 160 to 200 cm. Its natural range is limited to south-eastern and south-western Australia, in fresh, brackish and salt water with submerged and emergent vegetation; populations elsewhere (including New Zealand and Europe) were introduced. The diet is almost entirely vegetable, including reedmace, pondweed and algae. The clutch is usually 4 to 8 eggs and incubation takes 35 to 48 days. The IUCN lists it as Least Concern with a stable trend; specific threats are scarce apart from occasional illegal shooting.
