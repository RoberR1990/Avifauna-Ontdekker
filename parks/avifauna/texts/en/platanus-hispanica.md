## kind

This is not an animal but a tree! The plane tree loses pieces of its bark, a bit like a snake sheds its skin. That makes the trunk look patchy, grey with green and yellow. Its big leaves look like a hand with fingers. Can you find one?

## b1

The London plane is a big tree you often see in cities. It can grow 20 to 40 metres tall. Its leaves have three to five points and are glossy green. The bark is greyish brown and comes off in flakes. That leaves green and yellow patches on the trunk. The tree is a cross between two species: one from the east and one from America. It copes well with dirty air and little room for its roots. That is why it is planted along so many streets.

## kenner

Platanus × hispanica (also called Platanus × acerifolia) is a hybrid of the Oriental plane (Platanus orientalis) and the American sycamore (Platanus occidentalis), arisen in the seventeenth century. The tree grows 20 to 40 m tall. The palmately lobed leaves are glossy green above, and the greyish-brown bark exfoliates in patches. The fruit is a spherical head that breaks up slowly over winter. The tree tolerates air pollution, limited root space and heavy pruning well, and is therefore widely planted in cities.
