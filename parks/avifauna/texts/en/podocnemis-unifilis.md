## b1

The yellow-spotted river turtle lives in the big rivers of South America, such as the Amazon. He swims in calm water and in flooded forests. He has a brown or black shell and yellow spots on his head. These are easy to see, especially in young animals. He mostly eats plants and fruit. The female lays her eggs in a nest in the sand. The nest is warm, and that decides whether the baby is a boy or a girl. This turtle is vulnerable, because people eat it and its eggs.

## kenner

Podocnemis unifilis is a side-necked turtle (Podocnemididae) from the Amazon and Orinoco basins and the rivers of the Guianas. Females grow much larger than males and reach a shell length of up to about 45 centimetres. The species eats mainly plant material, supplemented with fish and carrion. Several clutches are laid per season; the sex of the young depends on nest temperature, with males below 32 degrees and females above. Incubation lasts from about two to five months. The IUCN classes the species as Vulnerable, after a long period of decline caused by the collection of meat and eggs and pressure on nesting sites.
