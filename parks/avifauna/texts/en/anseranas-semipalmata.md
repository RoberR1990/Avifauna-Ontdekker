## kind

This goose wears black and white and has orange legs. He likes to wade through shallow water. Sometimes two mothers and one father look after the chicks together. That is very unusual for a goose!

## b1

The magpie goose lives in northern Australia and southern New Guinea. It lives in open wetlands and on flooded grasslands. There it wades and swims, and it mostly eats grass, grass seeds and wild rice. Its feathers are black and white and its legs are orange. The female lays 3 to 9 eggs. Often one male pairs with two females, and all three help raise the young. The species is doing well now, but salt water creeping into freshwater swamps because of climate change is a danger.

## kenner

Anseranas semipalmata occurs in northern Australia and southern New Guinea, in open wetlands such as floodplains and coastal swamps. The bird is 70 to 90 cm long, weighs roughly 2,000 to 3,000 g and has a wingspan of 130 to 180 cm. The diet consists of grass, grass seeds, bulbs of wetland plants and wild rice. The clutch is 3 to 9 eggs, incubation is put at 24 to 35 days, and the species has an unusual breeding system with trios of one male and two females. The IUCN lists it as Least Concern with a stable trend, although its range is much smaller than it once was and its regional status within Australia varies (vulnerable or endangered in some states). Salt water intruding into freshwater floodplains is the main current concern.
