## b1

The ostrich lives in Africa, on the savanna and in dry areas. He is the biggest bird in the world. A grown-up ostrich is well over one and a half metres tall, and a male can grow to almost three metres. He cannot fly, but he can run, much faster than a human. He eats seeds, grass, fruit and insects. Several females lay their eggs in the same nest. There are still plenty of ostriches, but numbers are going down.

## kenner

Struthio camelus belongs to the family Struthionidae and the ratites (Palaeognathae). It lives in open savanna and Sahel in Africa, north and south of the rainforest. The birds lay 15 to 60 eggs in a communal nest, with an incubation period of 35 to 45 days; an egg weighs about 1.4 kilos. Adults weigh 90 to 130 kilos and can reach 70 kilometres an hour in short sprints. The IUCN classes the species as Least Concern, although the population is declining; the decline is not fast enough for a higher category. Habitat loss is the main threat, while hunting for feathers and skin used to weigh heavily.
