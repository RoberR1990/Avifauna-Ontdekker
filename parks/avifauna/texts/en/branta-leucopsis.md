## kind

This goose has a white face and a black neck. He often nests high up on a steep rock. So how do the chicks get down? They simply jump! They are so light and soft that they usually do not get hurt.

## b1

The barnacle goose breeds in the far north, on Greenland, Svalbard and in northern Russia. Its nest is on small islands or high on cliffs. The chicks cannot fly yet and jump down from the cliff. Thanks to their low weight and thick down this usually goes well. In winter the barnacle goose visits places such as the British Isles and the Netherlands. It eats grass, water plants and farm crops. The female usually lays 3 to 6 eggs, which are incubated for 24 to 26 days.

## kenner

Branta leucopsis is 55 to 70 cm long, weighs 1,210 to 2,230 g and has a wingspan of 120 to 145 cm. There are several separate breeding populations, on Greenland, Svalbard, in the Russian Arctic and around the Baltic. It nests on small islands and on rock faces up to 200 to 300 m high; the downy, lightweight chicks jump down after hatching. The clutch is usually 3 to 6 eggs, incubation takes 24 to 26 days, and only half of the chicks survive the first month. Lifespan is 18 to 25 years in the wild and 24 to 30 years in captivity. The species is Least Concern with an increasing trend; the medieval myth that the birds grew from barnacle shells persisted into the 18th century.
