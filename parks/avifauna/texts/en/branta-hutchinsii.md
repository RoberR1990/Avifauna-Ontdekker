## kind

This goose looks like a Canada goose, but he is smaller. He has a black head and neck with a white band under his chin. The smallest kind weighs only about 1.4 kg. That is less than any Canada goose!

## b1

The cackling goose breeds in northern Canada and Alaska, on the tundra. In winter it travels south, as far as northern Mexico. It mostly eats plants and grain from fields, and sometimes insects or crustaceans. It looks like the Canada goose but is smaller: 63 to 65 cm long. Its head and neck are black with a white band under the chin. The species is doing well and its numbers are growing. Foxes and coyotes sometimes eat the eggs and young geese.

## kenner

Branta hutchinsii is the smaller relative of the Canada goose, Branta canadensis, and measures 63 to 65 cm. Weight ranges from 1,400 to 2,380 g and wingspan from 108 to 111 cm. There are several subspecies; the smallest is B. h. minima from western Alaska, which weighs about 1.4 kg and occurs in the Netherlands as a non-migratory exotic. The species breeds on tundra in northern Canada and Alaska and winters across much of the United States, western Canada and northern Mexico. The diet is plant-based, supplemented with field grain, insects, molluscs and crustaceans. The IUCN status is Least Concern with an increasing trend after hunting laws and habitat restoration let numbers recover; predation by foxes, coyotes and feral animals remains a local factor.
