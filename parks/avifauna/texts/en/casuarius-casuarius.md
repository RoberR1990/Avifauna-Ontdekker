## kind

Can you see his blue neck and the red wattles? This is a southern cassowary. He is as tall as a grown-up. He cannot fly, but he can run. Did you know he can race through the forest at almost 50 kilometres an hour?

## b1

The southern cassowary lives in the rainforest of New Guinea and north-eastern Australia. He is a big bird that cannot fly. He grows to about 150 to 180 centimetres tall. His feathers are black, and his neck is blue and red. On top of his head there is a brown helmet. He mostly eats fruit that has fallen on the ground. He also eats seeds, mushrooms, insects and small animals.

## kenner

Casuarius casuarius is a ratite of the family Casuariidae, living in lowland rainforest in New Guinea and north-eastern Australia. Females are larger than males: they weigh up to about 76 kg, males up to about 55 kg. The diet consists mainly of fallen fruit, supplemented with seeds, fungi, insects and small vertebrates. According to different sources the clutch is three to eight eggs; incubation lasts about 50 days (47 to 61 are reported). Worldwide the species is classed as Least Concern on the Red List, with a decreasing trend, and the Australian population is considered Endangered. Habitat loss, vehicle collisions and feral dogs and pigs all play a part.
