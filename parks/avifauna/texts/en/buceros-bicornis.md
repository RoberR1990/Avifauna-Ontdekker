## kind

Can you see the big yellow bump on top of his beak? That is his helmet. He lives in the rainforest and mostly eats fruit. Did you know the female nests in a tree hole? She walls herself in, and the male passes her food through a narrow gap!

## b1

The great hornbill comes from Asia. He lives in old rainforest in India, Southeast Asia and Sumatra. He is a big bird, about one metre long. He mostly eats figs and other fruit. Sometimes he also catches insects or small animals. On his beak there is a hollow yellow helmet. The female nests in a hollow tree, where she is walled in. The bird is in trouble, because the old forest is being cut down.

## kenner

Buceros bicornis is a large hornbill (Bucerotidae) with a hollow casque whose function is unknown; males are said to bump into each other in mid-air with it during the breeding season. It is mainly frugivorous, preferring fat-rich fruit such as figs, supplemented with small vertebrates and insects. The female incubates one or two eggs in a cavity in an old tree, which she seals up almost completely with a layer of plaster; the male feeds her through a narrow slit during the roughly 38 to 40 days of incubation. Because the species relies on old trees with cavities, logging hits it directly. Since 2018 it has been listed as Vulnerable on the Red List, with a decreasing trend, and it is covered by CITES Appendix I.
